# Shadow Co-Author — A Protocol for Preserving Tacit Knowledge

[![License](https://img.shields.io/badge/license-CC%20BY--SA%204.0-blue)](LICENSE)
[![Status](https://img.shields.io/badge/status-active-brightgreen)]()

**Maxim:**
A system that outlives its creator inevitably loses their unspoken meanings. Explicit knowledge passes on the manual. Tacit knowledge passes on the spirit. The Architect is obliged to leave both.

---

### 1. Diagnosis

Formal documentation captures *what* and *how*. Specifications describe the logic. Protocols describe the procedures. But no document answers the question: «Why was this particular decision made, at that moment, under those circumstances?» Knowledge of context, of rejected alternatives, of the creator's aesthetic and ethical preferences remains invisible.

When the Architect departs, this non-formalized part of the system disappears with them. Heirs receive a working mechanism, but lose the key to its spirit. Further development follows one of two paths:

- **Dogmatic ossification.** Heirs fear changing anything because they do not understand the boundaries of what is permissible. The system becomes a monument, not a living tool.
- **Uncontrolled mutation.** Heirs boldly change the system, guided by their own understanding, and imperceptibly destroy its architectural integrity.

The Shadow Co-Author is a discipline that turns loss from a catastrophe into a managed process. It does not demand the impossible from the Architect — to express the inexpressible. It demands leaving behind a «shadow» — a system of pointers that will help heirs make decisions in the spirit of the design, even when the Architect themself can no longer advise.

---

### 2. Axiomatics

1.  **Tacit knowledge is just as much a system component as code or a specification.** Its loss is equivalent to the removal of a critical module without documentation.
2.  **Complete transmission is impossible.** The Architect cannot pour their experience, intuition, and sense of beauty into the heads of their heirs. But they can leave a map that will indicate direction in moments of doubt.
3.  **The spirit of a system is passed on not through dogma, but through principles of interpretation.** The task is not for heirs to repeat the creator's decisions, but for them to make their own decisions without destroying the architectural design.

---

### 3. Protocols of the Shadow Co-Author

#### 3.1. Decision Journal
Every key architectural decision should be accompanied by a record containing:

- **Context.** What was known at the moment of the decision? What constraints were in effect?
- **Alternatives.** What other options were considered and why were they rejected?
- **Cost.** What was sacrificed for the sake of this choice?

A decision journal is not a report on successes. It is a chronicle of doubts and compromises. It is this that gives heirs an understanding of the logic, not just its result.

#### 3.2. Doubt Stack
The Architect maintains a registry of open questions to which they have not found an answer. This may be a list of unresolved contradictions, unfinished forks, hypotheses awaiting verification.

The Doubt Stack performs two functions:
- Honestly tells heirs: «Here I did not know the right way. You will have to decide for yourselves.»
- Prevents dogmatization: if the creator doubted, then their decisions were not the ultimate truth.

#### 3.3. Interpretation Safeguards
The Architect must explicitly mark the boundaries within which the system can be changed without losing its spirit, and the red lines whose crossing will destroy the architectural design.

- **What can be changed freely** (implementation details, secondary parameters).
- **What can be changed with caution** (core protocols, provided their spirit is preserved).
- **What must never be changed** (the axiomatic core, the basic ethical principles of the system).

Interpretation safeguards are not shackles. They are guardrails that allow heirs to move faster and more safely.

#### 3.4. Aesthetic Principles of the System
Any deep system has its own aesthetics. The Architect should record what they consider beautiful, harmonious, and proportionate within their creation. This can be expressed in:

- Key metaphors (e.g., «The Thread Between Heaven and Earth» for IPIS).
- Principles of simplicity, or, conversely, of permissible complexity.
- Examples of what the Architect considers «ugly» or alien to the system.

Aesthetic principles are the most subtle, but also the longest-living layer of tacit knowledge. Technical solutions become obsolete. Metaphors and beauty live for centuries.

#### 3.5. Shadow Double
If the scale of the mission allows, the Architect should raise a living carrier of tacit knowledge — someone who understands the spirit of the system not from documents, but through joint work. Ideally, there should be several such people.

A Shadow Double is not just a student. It is someone who:
- Was present when key decisions were made.
- Knows the context that did not make it into the journals.
- Is capable of interpreting the system for the next generation of heirs.

If the Architect departs without leaving anyone who «breathed the same air» as their design, the mission loses a vital transmission channel.

---

### 4. Tacit Knowledge Metrics

| Metric | Code | Question to ask oneself | What to watch for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Decision Journal Completeness | DJ | Have key decisions been documented with context and alternatives? | If not, heirs will receive a manual without the logic |
| Doubt Stack Depth | DS | Have open questions and rejected forks been recorded? | An empty stack is a sign that the creator either knew everything (impossible) or remained silent |
| Presence of Interpretation Safeguards | IS | Have the boundaries of permissible changes and red lines been marked? | Without them, heirs will either freeze the system or break it |
| Fixation of Aesthetic Principles | AP | Has what is beautiful and what is alien to the system been expressed in words or images? | Without this, the most enduring layer of spirit is lost |
| Presence of a Shadow Double | TD | Is there a person who understands the spirit of the system not from documents, but through experience? | If not, the transmission channel is knowingly incomplete |

---

### 5. Applicability

This protocol is recommended for everyone who creates systems, ideas, or meanings that are designed to live longer than the period of their active participation. It does not demand literary talent or philosophical education. It demands only the discipline of recording and honesty before the future.
